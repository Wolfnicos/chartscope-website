## How to Identify Key Indicators Without Complexity

### Start With Just Two Indicators

Here's the mistake most beginners make: they try to learn every indicator at once. Stochastic, Bollinger Bands, Ichimoku, Fibonacci — the list never ends. The result is paralysis by analysis.

**Start with RSI and MACD.** These two indicators give you roughly 80% of the information you need for basic trend analysis. The RSI measures the speed and change of price movements on a scale of 0 to 100. When it's above 70, the asset may be overbought. Below 30, it may be oversold.

MACD shows you the relationship between two moving averages. When the MACD line crosses above the signal line, it's a bullish signal. Below — bearish. That's the whole concept.

### The 3-Step Indicator Checklist

Follow this exact process every time you open a chart:

1. **Check the trend first** — Look at the overall direction. Is the chart making higher highs and higher lows (uptrend) or lower highs and lower lows (downtrend)?
2. **Apply RSI** — Is the reading above 70 or below 30? If yes, the move may be exhausted.
3. **Confirm with MACD** — Has the MACD line recently crossed the signal line? This confirms momentum direction.

This three-step process takes less than 60 seconds and gives you a solid foundation for any trading decision. The key is consistency — do it every time, not just when you remember.

## Common Pitfalls When Learning Technical Analysis

### Pitfall #1: Indicator Overload

The most common mistake is trying to use too many indicators at once. Each indicator adds noise, not clarity. **The fix:** Limit yourself to RSI and MACD for your first 90 days. Master those before adding anything else.

### Pitfall #2: Ignoring the Timeframe

A pattern on a 5-minute chart is noise. The same pattern on a daily chart is significant. Beginners often jump between timeframes without understanding this distinction. **The fix:** Pick one timeframe (daily is ideal for learning) and stick with it for at least two weeks.

### Pitfall #3: Confusing Education with Signals

This is where most "free education" tools fail. They give you signals — buy this, sell that — without teaching you why. When you don't understand the reasoning, you can't adapt when conditions change. **The fix:** Use tools that explain, not just signal. ChartScope's AI Chat explicitly never gives signals — it always explains why. That's the educational approach.

### Pitfall #4: Skipping the "Why"

"RSI is above 70, so sell" is not understanding. "RSI is above 70 because buying momentum has been strong but may be exhausting itself" is understanding. **The fix:** After every analysis, write one sentence explaining *why* the indicator is in its current state. If you can't, you haven't learned it yet.
