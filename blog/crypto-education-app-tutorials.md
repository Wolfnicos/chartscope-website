## Step 1: Master Technical Analysis Fundamentals


<figure style="margin:1.5rem 0 2rem 0;"><img src="https://pub-2df00e47a1bc4a99abcb17ec68f147ce.r2.dev/hive/body/185ae491-3ba7-467f-b13b-c1c33111559e.png" alt="Step 1: Master Technical Analysis Fundamentals" style="width:100%;max-height:420px;object-fit:cover;border-radius:8px;display:block;"></figure>

Before executing your first trade, you must understand how to read market sentiment through price charts. Focus on these three pillars:
1. **Candlestick Patterns:** Learn to identify reversals and continuations (e.g., Doji, Engulfing patterns).
2. **Support and Resistance:** Identify key price levels where buying or selling pressure historically intensifies.
3. **Volume Analysis:** Confirm price moves by ensuring they are backed by significant trading volume.

## Step 3: Develop a Risk Management Strategy


<figure style="margin:1.5rem 0 2rem 0;"><img src="https://pub-2df00e47a1bc4a99abcb17ec68f147ce.r2.dev/hive/body/45f1a57e-8b8f-4b9e-92c5-d3d42502fb9a.png" alt="Step 3: Develop a Risk Management Strategy" style="width:100%;max-height:420px;object-fit:cover;border-radius:8px;display:block;"></figure>

Trading is 20% analysis and 80% risk management. Even the most accurate technical analysis will fail if your position sizing is incorrect. 
* **The 1% Rule:** Never risk more than 1% of your total portfolio on a single trade.
* **Stop-Loss Placement:** Always define your exit point before entering a position.
* **Risk-to-Reward Ratio:** Aim for a minimum 1:2 ratio, ensuring your potential gains outweigh your potential losses.

## Step 4: Backtesting and Journaling
Consistency is built through review. Keep a trading journal to document your emotional state, the technical setup used, and the final outcome of every trade. By reviewing your history, you can identify patterns in your decision-making process and refine your strategy over time.
